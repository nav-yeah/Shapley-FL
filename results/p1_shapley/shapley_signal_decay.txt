============================================================
source: shapley_scores.csv   (1500 rows, 30 clients, 50 rounds)

Truncation at P1's current threshold (eps_b = 0.02):
  all-zero (truncated) rounds : 38/50 (76%)
  Shapley-live rounds         : 12/50 (24%)
  live round numbers          : [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 15, 17, 21, 22]
  permanent collapse from round 23: every round from 23 to 50 is truncated
  [check] sweep reproduces the observed truncation count at eps_b=0.02 (38 rounds) — gain reconstruction verified.

Why the signal dies:
  mean |gain|, rounds 2-10          : 0.03895
  mean |gain|, second half of run   : 0.00257
  ratio                             : 15.2x
  The threshold is an ABSOLUTE accuracy delta. It was
  calibrated on a 5-round run where every round gained
  several points. After convergence the same rule discards
  every round, so the valuation signal is thrown away
  exactly where per-client differences become subtle.

  For reference: on a 2074-sample test set (detected from preprocessed/har_clients.pkl), one sample = 0.00048 accuracy.
  Because v0 and vN are evaluated on the SAME fixed test set
  with two highly correlated models, the meaningful noise
  floor is the paired-disagreement scale (a handful of
  samples), not the independent-sample standard error.
  eps_b = 0.0035 (~7 samples) is the proposed floor.

Handoff notes:
  P2: truncated rounds fall back to size-proportional
      weights, so Shapley weighting is inactive in them.
      Report the live-round count alongside any accuracy
      comparison, or the comparison is misleading.
  P3: exclude truncated rounds from eligible-round counts
      before applying the sustained-deviation ratio. A
      client cannot deviate from its history in a round
      where nobody has a score.
  P4: all-zero rounds still produce on-chain entries that
      carry no valuation content; worth noting in the audit
      trail description.
